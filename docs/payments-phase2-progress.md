## 2026-10-26 — Validate the UK low-value VAT gate against HMRC guidance

- **Research:** current HMRC guidance distinguishes direct overseas sales from sales through an online marketplace. For goods imported to Great Britain from outside the UK, the £135 test is the total import consignment's intrinsic value (not each item); a qualifying direct sale has VAT charged at sale, and the seller must know the goods' precise nature/rate and be VAT registered. For a qualifying online-marketplace sale, the marketplace is liable for point-of-sale VAT and must likewise determine the correct rate and keep records. HMRC defines an online marketplace by its involvement in setting supply terms, authorising/facilitating payment, and ordering/delivery. Sources: [direct sales guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-directly-to-customers-in-the-uk) and [online marketplace guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-to-customers-in-the-uk-using-online-marketplaces).
- **Implication:** the implemented country readiness booleans are only a gate; they do not establish who legally accounts for VAT, the correct garment/supply classification or rate, the consignment value, or whether Drapeon meets HMRC's online-marketplace definition for the transaction. HMRC also distinguishes Great Britain from Northern Ireland for these imports, while the current delivery-rule key is country-level (`GB`); a single GB boolean cannot encode both treatments. Do not infer a generic VAT rate or enable the UK delivery rule from the £135 threshold alone. Add seller/platform classification, goods origin/location, consignment aggregation, and GB-vs-NI treatment to legal/tax review and require a reviewed tax decision snapshot before any UK route can be enabled.
